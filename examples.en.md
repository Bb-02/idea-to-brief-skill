# Example requests

[简体中文](examples.md) | **English** · [Back to README](README.en.md)

**These are hand-written example requests, not real conversations, AI outputs, or test results.** Copy one or adapt it to your topic. For a real user report, see the [book-club test in the README](README.en.md#user-test-one-request-parallel-research).

Each scenario offers two choices: **ask for a prompt** to give to the current AI or another one, or **ask for findings** and have the current AI investigate.

## 1. Find projects and inspiration

For exploring ideas, a short list with basic screening is enough.

**Design a search task:**

```text
I'd like to discover interesting recent apps by solo developers,
on both mobile and desktop. I want inspiration, not an in-depth review of each app.
Turn this into a search task I can give to another AI.
```

**Search directly:**

```text
Find some recent apps by solo developers worth exploring, on mobile and desktop.
Include less well-known projects. Give me a short list with what makes each
interesting and links to the sources.
```

## 2. Investigate practical approaches

For promoting a project, focus on what others tried, the evidence, and what you can try yourself.

**Design an investigation:**

```text
I built an open-source app on my own. I have no audience and don't know
where to promote it. I want to learn how others found their first users.
Write a practical research prompt I can give to another AI.
```

**Investigate directly:**

```text
Investigate how solo developers with no existing audience promote open-source apps.
Find real cases. Tell me where to go, what to prepare, and how to follow up,
with a workload one person can manage. Be more specific than “create content”
or “build a community,” and include sources for your recommendations.
```

## 3. Compare technical approaches in depth

Before choosing a project direction, compare options across use cases to understand the tradeoffs.

**Design a research task:**

```text
I want to research game engines for a solo developer or a team of up to three.
I haven't chosen a game genre. I care about how much AI coding can actually help,
and I want to examine real small-team projects and failures.
Write a complete research task for another AI, without picking a single winner yet.
```

**Research directly:**

```text
Compare game engines for solo developers and teams of up to three in depth.
Use current official documentation, real small-team projects, and practical
AI coding workflows. Explain tradeoffs by game type and include sources.
Don't choose an engine for me yet.
```

## After receiving a task description

**Narrow the scope and revise it:**

```text
The scope is too broad. Limit it to Windows, with a simple search and screening.
Give me the complete revised task description.
```

**Start the investigation:**

```text
Now carry out this task and give me the findings and sources.
```
